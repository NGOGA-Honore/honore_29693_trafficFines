## Problem Statement

Traffic fines are issued at the roadside or by camera, but they are usually paid later through a separate channel. Because issuing and paying are not tied together by one set of enforced rules, unpaid fines, repeat offenders and late penalties are hard to see.

The evidence shows the scale of the problem:

- In a study of 3,571,341 electronic challans in Ahmedabad (India), 2,489,209 (about 70%) were unpaid, and users who paid took 153 days on average (Srikanth et al., 2020).
- In Rwanda, more than 409,000 drivers were fined for speeding in 2024-2025 (Ntirenganya, 2026). Fines must be paid within 30 days, after which a late fee applies (Rwanda National Police, n.d.).
- The Rwanda National Police and IremboGov pages describe checking and paying a fine. They do not describe arrears tracking, and once a fine is paid it is no longer linked to the vehicle (IremboGov, n.d.).

TrafficFineTrack is an Oracle PL/SQL database that connects every violation to its fine and every payment to its fine. Its rules are enforced inside the database, so records cannot be duplicated, overpaid or silently changed.

## Objectives

**Main objective:** Build an Oracle PL/SQL database that records traffic violations, issues fines and tracks payments, so that unpaid fines and repeat offenders are detected early and every change is auditable.

**Specific objectives:**

1. Design a normalized (3NF) schema for drivers, vehicles, violation types, violations, fines, payments, system parameters and an audit log, and load it with real violation records.
2. Enforce the business rules in PL/SQL: block duplicate tickets, block payments above the amount owed, and apply a late-payment surcharge after the due date.
3. Detect repeat offenders (violations in a rolling 12-month window) and produce an arrears report of overdue fines with a cursor-based procedure.
4. Record every change to fines and payments in an audit log, and block edits to payment history.
5. Provide a simple Streamlit application on top of the database so an officer can record a violation and a cashier can record a payment through the PL/SQL procedures.

## Data Source

- **Dataset:** Montgomery County of Maryland, *Traffic Violations*, electronic traffic violations issued in the county. Published by the county as open data and updated daily.
- **Link:** https://data.montgomerycountymd.gov/d/4mse-ku6q (Data.gov record: https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/traffic-violations)
- **Size:** the published 2015-2017 extract documented in the `exampledata` R package has 1,276,580 rows and 35 variables (trinker, n.d.). The live dataset is larger. The project loads a subset of a few thousand to tens of thousands of rows.
- **Columns used:** Date Of Stop, Time Of Stop, Latitude, Longitude, Accident, Fatal, State, VehicleType, Year, Make, Model, Color, Violation Type (Warning or Citation) and Charge. Only records with Violation Type = Citation become fines.
- **Personal information:** the publisher states that information that can uniquely identify the vehicle, the vehicle owner or the issuing officer is not published. I also drop Race, Gender and Driver City. Driver and vehicle keys in my database are generated surrogate keys, so no real person is identified.
- **Licence:** the Data.gov record does not state a licence. The data is public open government data, used here for study only.
- **Limitations:** the dataset records violations only, so it has no fine amounts and no payments. Fine amounts come from a fine schedule table that I define, and payments are generated by the system's own procedures (simulated). I did not find a public violation-level dataset for Rwanda, so this US county dataset is a structural stand-in. See `data/README.md`.

## Innovation

**Option:** User interface.
**Tool:** Streamlit (Python) connected to the Oracle database with python-oracledb. An officer records a violation and a cashier records a payment; the application calls the PL/SQL procedures `record_violation` and `record_payment`, and shows live arrears and repeat-offender lists. The business rules stay in PL/SQL; the application only shows and sends data.

## Planned Tables

| Table | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `driver` | Generated driver keys (state only, no personal data) |
| `vehicle` | Vehicle details linked to a driver |
| `violation_type` | Charge code, description, severity and base fine (the fine schedule) |
| `violation` | Each violation event: date, time, location, vehicle, charge, warning or citation |
| `fine` | One fine per cited violation: amount, issue date, due date (issue date + 30 days), status |
| `payment` | Payments against fines: amount, date, channel |
| `system_parameter` | Configurable rules: grace days, late-surcharge percentage, repeat-offender threshold |
| `audit_log` | Who changed what and when on fines and payments |

Relationships: driver 1-to-many vehicle; vehicle 1-to-many violation; violation_type 1-to-many violation; violation 1-to-1 fine; fine 1-to-many payment.

## Planned PL/SQL Features

| Feature | Where it is used |
|---|---|
| Procedure `record_violation` | Inserts a violation and, for a citation, issues the fine from the fine schedule |
| Procedure `record_payment` | Records a payment and updates the fine status (partial or paid) |
| Procedure `generate_arrears_report` | Uses a cursor to loop over unpaid fines past their due date and mark them overdue |
| Function `calc_late_surcharge` | Returns the surcharge owed on a fine after its due date |
| Function `is_repeat_offender` | Counts a driver's violations in the last 12 months against the threshold |
| Package `traffic_fine_pkg` | Groups the procedures and functions above |
| Trigger (before insert on `violation`) | Blocks a duplicate ticket for the same vehicle, charge, date and time |
| Trigger (before insert on `payment`) | Blocks a payment above the amount still owed or on an already paid fine |
| Trigger (after insert or update on `fine` and `payment`) | Writes every change to `audit_log` |
| Restriction trigger (before update or delete on `payment`) | Blocks changes to payment history |
| Custom exceptions | `e_duplicate_ticket` and `e_overpayment` return clear error messages to the application |
| `DBMS_SCHEDULER` job (optional) | Runs the overdue check nightly |
